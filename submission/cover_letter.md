To the Editors, *Nature Cancer*

Dear Editors,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "A bile-duct CD8/CXCR3 axis defines steroid-resistant checkpoint-inhibitor hepatitis," for consideration as a Brief Communication.

Immune-checkpoint-inhibitor (ICI) hepatitis is among the most frequent dose-limiting immune-related adverse events of cancer immunotherapy. A clinically distinctive subset is refractory to corticosteroids and progresses towards bile-duct injury, yet second-line management (for example, mycophenolate mofetil) is chosen empirically, with no biology-driven rationale specific to steroid-resistant disease (SR-irHep). Resolving the tissue biology of this subset is a direct, unmet need for the growing population treated with checkpoint blockade.

Using 10x Xenium single-cell spatial transcriptomics of steroid-exposed SR-irHep liver biopsies, with treatment-naïve type I autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) as an immune-mediated comparator, we identify a bile-duct-centred CD8 architecture engaging MHC-I and CXCR3 in SR-irHep that inverts, in both cellular geometry and ligand–receptor grammar, the parenchyma-centred CD4 architecture of AIH. This axis provides a cellular rationale for the empirical use of mycophenolate, aligns most closely with JAK1/2 inhibition (ruxolitinib) among the reported second-line options, and nominates CXCR3 as a candidate second-line target. To our knowledge, this is the first spatial-transcriptomic candidate target for SR-irHep.

The study profiles four biopsies — two per condition — at single-cell spatial resolution; we present it candidly as a hypothesis-generating cohort. The therapeutic implications are framed as hypotheses for dedicated preclinical evaluation, and the spatial architectures as computational predictions requiring independent-cohort and protein-level confirmation. We nonetheless believe the conceptual advance — a reproducible inversion of the effector–target architecture between steroid-resistant ICI hepatitis and autoimmune hepatitis that nominates a candidate second-line target — is of broad interest to the *Nature Cancer* readership at the interface of tumour immunology and immunotherapy toxicity.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and has been read and approved by all authors.
