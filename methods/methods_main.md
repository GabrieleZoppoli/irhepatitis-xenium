# Methods

## Patients, samples and ethics

Liver biopsies from four patients were profiled at the Azienda Ospedaliera Metropolitana IRCCS Ospedale Policlinico San Martino (Genoa, Italy) as a retrospective small case series: two patients with steroid-resistant immune-checkpoint-inhibitor (ICI) hepatitis (SR-irHep; sample identifiers 18321_a, 24774_d) following PD-1 blockade and second-line corticosteroid failure, and two patients with treatment-naïve autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) type I, diagnosed clinically and serologically per international AIH simplified criteria (sample identifiers 54023_b, 56784_c). Sample identifiers are pseudonymous codes; the linkage to the source patient is held only at the institutional level. All four patients had provided written informed consent for the research use and publication of de-identified clinical and biological material under the institutional research-consent framework in place at the Azienda Ospedaliera Metropolitana IRCCS Ospedale Policlinico San Martino. Under Italian regulations on retrospective observational research at IRCCS institutions (AIFA Determina 20 March 2008; Decreto Ministeriale 30 November 2021; DGR Liguria 858/2023), a dedicated retrospective study protocol was not required for this small case series; all procedures followed the policies of the ethics committee of Regione Liguria in the matter of publication of case reports and/or case series. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) as implemented in Italian law (D.Lgs. 196/2003 as amended by D.Lgs. 101/2018). Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks were sectioned at 5 µm onto Xenium slides per the 10× Genomics manufacturer's protocol.

## Xenium spatial transcriptomics

*In situ* profiling was performed on the 10× Genomics Xenium Analyzer with the 380-gene Human Immuno-Oncology Panel. Standard vendor protocols were followed for slide preparation, hybridisation, decoding, and cell segmentation (cell + nuclear boundaries from DAPI plus interior cell-membrane staining). After Xenium acquisition, slides were stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E), and the resulting whole-slide images were registered to the Xenium coordinate space using the vendor `he_alignment/matrix.csv` affine transform. Per-sample output (cell-feature matrix, transcripts, cell and nucleus boundaries, DAPI and H&E pyramids, gene panel JSON) was retained in 10× standard layout. Per-sample vendor QC metrics (transcripts per cell, cells per area, segmentation rate) are reported in Supplementary Table S2; all four samples passed vendor reference ranges.

## Data processing, integration and clustering

Per-sample cell-feature matrices were imported into the Giotto framework (v4.2.2). Cell-level QC retained cells with at least 4 transcripts; genes were retained if detected in at least 3 cells; transcripts with decoding quality QV < 20 were discarded. Counts were normalised by log-transformed library-size scaling (scale factor 5,000). The four samples were embedded with PCA (50 components computed), batch-corrected with Harmony (theta = 2, sigma = 0.1, lambda = 1) on the first 15 principal components, projected with UMAP (15 nearest neighbours, min_dist = 0.0005), and clustered with the Leiden algorithm (k = 15, resolution 0.5). Twenty-one primary clusters were obtained. Three were excluded after pathology-aware quality review: one four-cell sample-specific outlier (cluster 21); a fragment cluster with median cell area ≤ 20 µm² and IGKC as the dominant detected marker, consistent with segmentation artefacts (cluster 12); and a hepatocyte-marker cluster restricted to a poorly preserved tissue region (cluster 15). Eighteen active clusters across 146,014 cells were retained. Cluster identity was assigned from per-cluster marker genes (Giotto `findMarkers_one_vs_all`, scran method, FDR < 0.05) against canonical liver-atlas signatures. Full top-5 markers and per-sample cell counts are in Supplementary Tables S2 and S3.

## Per-cluster differential expression

For cluster characterisation, per-cluster vs. all-other-clusters differential expression was computed with Giotto's wrapper around `scran::findMarkers` on per-cell log-normalised counts (Supplementary Table S3).

## Spatial proximity analysis (Figure 2A)

Pairwise cluster–cluster adjacency was quantified per sample with `Giotto::cellProximityEnrichment` on a Delaunay spatial graph of cell centroids. The enrichment statistic is log₂((observed + 1) / (expected + 1)), where the expected number of neighbours is drawn from 1,000 random permutations of cluster labels within each sample. Two one-sided empirical p-values per pair (attract, avoid) were computed and Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted within sample. To accommodate the small cohort size (n = 2 per condition), a *coherent rule* was applied per direction per condition: a pair was retained when at least one sample of the condition reached p<sub>adj</sub> < 0.10 in a given direction *and* no sample of the same condition crossed p<sub>adj</sub> < 0.10 in the opposite direction. The condition mean log₂ enrichment was computed across the two samples of each condition; the differential Δ(SR-irHep − AIH) was used as tile colour in Figure 2A. The 10 pairs with largest |Δ| under the coherent rule are outlined in the heatmap. A permutation-based supplementary analysis (1,000 cluster-label permutations across the four samples; Supplementary Table S7) confirms that the coherent signal is unlikely to arise by chance.

## Cell–cell communication (Figure 2B)

Per-sample ligand–receptor (L–R) communication probabilities were inferred with CellChat v2 (ref. 9) using a physical-contact neighbourhood (`contact.range` = 25 µm, `spot.size` = 10, `type = "truncatedMean"`, `trim` = 0.1) against the full CellChatDB. Three multi-ligand families whose CellChat defaults collapse mechanistically distinct receptors — MHC-I, CXCL and CCL — were ligand-split into receptor-specific axes (e.g. MHC-I_CD8A, CXCL_CXCR3, CCL_CCR1) before aggregation, yielding the receptor-anchored pathway labels shown in the Sankey. Per-condition mean communication probabilities were log₂-transformed with a pseudo-count and the differential Δ(SR-irHep − AIH) computed. The same coherent rule used in the proximity analysis was applied to the L–R × cell-pair triple: a flow was retained when at least one sample of the enriched condition had non-zero probability and no sample of the opposite condition exceeded that probability on the same triple. Forty-seven coherent triples passed. The 10 strongest per direction by |log₂FC| are shown in Figure 2B, with clusters of < 10 cells in any sample excluded (this single filter removed the Ig⁺ hepatocyte cluster, with 2 cells in sample 24774_d; all other clusters retained ≥ 28 cells per sample). The full coherent set is in Supplementary Table S5.

For Figure 2B display, MHC-I_CD8A and MHC-I_CD8B flows in which CellChat had assigned a CD8 cluster as source were redrawn with the presenter on the source axis and the CD8⁺ T cell on the target axis, in keeping with the biology of antigen presentation; flows with the non-T cell already in the source column were left untouched. Both raw and redrawn directions are reported in Supplementary Table S5.

## Orthogonal validation: LIANA+ and COMMOT

CellChat findings were cross-validated by two independent computational approaches operating on the same per-sample data. *Expression-only consensus.* Per-sample expression-only communication scoring was performed with LIANA+ (ref. 10) using five orthogonal scoring methods — NATMI, Connectome, logFC, SingleCellSignalR and CellPhoneDB — against the LIANA Consensus database with Robust Rank Aggregation; cross-method per-pair ranks are in Supplementary Table S6. *Spatial optimal transport.* Per-sample spatial-aware CCC was computed with COMMOT (ref. 11) using the CellChatDB ligand–receptor database, species = "human", cell-cell distance threshold `dis_thr` = 250 µm and heteromeric receptor handling enabled. COMMOT was run on each sample individually (the largest sample, 54023_b, with 52,266 cells × 33 testable L–R pairs, required ≈ 19 h on a 32-core / 376 GB workstation). Per-sample scores were aggregated to per-condition differentials with explicit three-layer handling of structural missingness (presence-only versus testable-zero versus untested); details are in the Supplementary Methods.

## Representative regions of interest (Figure 2C)

Four representative 120 × 120 µm regions of interest (ROIs) were rendered to ground the cohort-level findings in tissue: two anatomy rows (bile-duct, parenchymal) × two condition columns. ROIs were selected by pathology review of the four sections as representative of each condition × anatomy axis (bile-duct ROIs centred on a duct surrounded by lymphocytic infiltrate; parenchymal ROIs centred on a hepatocyte-dense field with lymphocytes in apposition). Coordinates of the selected ROIs (within each tissue, in Xenium image coordinates): bile-duct row, SR-irHep sample 18321_a (centre 1572, −2146) and AIH sample 54023_b (3195, −9694); parenchymal row, SR-irHep sample 18321_a (8228, −1175) and AIH sample 54023_b (3207, −9960).

Each row highlights the anatomy and its primary Panel-B partner: bile-duct row, cholangiocytes (gold) and the unified Chol-engaged CD8 subclusters CD8T2 + CD8T3 (medium/dark blue); parenchymal row, hepatocytes (red, four hepatocyte subclusters unified) and CD4⁺ T cells (mint). All other clusters appear as light-grey "Other cells". Cell and nucleus boundaries were taken from the Xenium `cell_boundaries.parquet` and `nucleus_boundaries.parquet` files; cluster identity per polygon was assigned by nearest-centroid match to the Giotto cell-feature object. Partner-lineage cells whose centroid lay within 10 µm of any anatomy-partner cell-boundary vertex were flagged "in contact" — these cells received a magenta (#AD1457) border and a jittered in-pane arrow pointing at them from outside the cell (4 µm tip clearance, 6 µm shaft, arrow head 0.12 in; yellow #FFD600 with black halo on H&E, white with black halo on the segmentation overlay). Arrow orientations were seeded per ROI (`set.seed(42 + ROI id)`) so the spatial pattern reproduces across renders.

Xenium-aligned H&E images were cropped per ROI by an affine-registration pipeline (`tifffile` + `scipy.ndimage.affine_transform`) using the vendor `he_alignment/matrix.csv`. Crops were contrast-normalised identically across all ROIs (`magick::image_contrast` followed by `image_modulate`). Cell-type-specific transcripts were filtered for `is_gene == TRUE` and Xenium decoding quality QV ≥ 20 and plotted as single-molecule dots within the ROI box without cell-membership filtering, so gene–cell specificity is visible directly. Genes shown: EPCAM, CXCL6 (cholangiocyte markers) and CD8A, KLRK1 (CD8 T-cell markers) on the bile-duct row; ARG1 (hepatocyte marker) and IL7R, CD2 (CD4 T-cell markers) on the parenchymal row.

Panel C is a visual illustration of the cohort-level proximity (Fig. 2A) and ligand–receptor (Fig. 2B) findings at single-cell scale; the inferential statistics underlying any condition-specific claim reside in Panels A and B and are not derived from Panel C.

## Software, data and code availability

All analyses were performed in R 4.5.2 (Giotto 4.2.2, GiottoClass, CellChat 2.x, ggalluvial, ggplot2, cowplot, magick, FNN, arrow, data.table) and Python 3.10+ (commot 0.0.3, anndata, tifffile, scipy.ndimage, PIL). Version-pinned conda environment specifications are at `scripts/env/xenium_env.yml` and `scripts/env/commot_env.yml`, with an exact R-package manifest (`scripts/env/R_session_packages.tsv`, including the GitHub commits for the Giotto suite, CellChat and LIANA+) and the full `sessionInfo` (`scripts/env/R_sessionInfo.txt`). Raw Xenium output has been deposited at the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) under accession GSE339299 (private during peer review, released on publication). Processed objects (Giotto session snapshot, per-sample CCC outputs) are archived at Zenodo (DOI: 10.5281/zenodo.21453827). Analysis scripts (data ingestion, clustering, proximity, CCC, ROI rendering, figure assembly) are at https://github.com/GabrieleZoppoli/irhepatitis-xenium under the MIT licence. The repository also contains the manuscript text and final figures under CC-BY-4.0.

A complete, sequential end-to-end pipeline launcher is at `scripts/run_full_pipeline.sh`. The pipeline is dominated by COMMOT runtime; an end-to-end re-run from raw Xenium output requires approximately one to two days of compute on a modern workstation. Random seeds are fixed for the Leiden clustering, the proximity-permutation step and ROI arrow jitter; all other steps are deterministic.
