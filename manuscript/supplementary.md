## 1. Supplementary Methods

### 1.1 Cross-method validation of cell–cell communication: extended detail

CellChat v2 inferences (Figure 2B; full coherent set in **Supplementary Table S5**) were cross-validated by two independent computational approaches operating on the same per-sample data.

**LIANA+ consensus.** Per-sample expression-only communication scoring was performed with LIANA+ (v1.x) using five orthogonal scoring methods (NATMI, Connectome, logFC, SingleCellSignalR (SCA) and CellPhoneDB) against the LIANA Consensus database with Robust Rank Aggregation; cross-method per-pair ranks are in **Supplementary Table S6**.

**COMMOT spatial optimal transport: three-layer missingness handling.** Per-sample COMMOT scores were aggregated to per-condition differentials with explicit three-layer handling of structural missingness:

1. L–R pair not testable in a sample because the per-cell expression filter (`min_cell_pct` = 0.05) failed in that sample → NA, not zero.
2. Source or target cluster present at fewer than 10 cells in a sample → NA.
3. Pair tested, both endpoints present, OT flow returned zero → 0.

Eleven L–R pairs were strictly comparable across all four samples; twelve additional pairs were AIH-only-testable and reported in **Supplementary Table S5** with the presence-based comparison flagged. **Supplementary Fig. S1** visualises COMMOT spatial signalling vectors over tissue for the two strongest SR-irHep-enriched axes. Cross-method agreement outcomes are reported in §2.1.

### 1.2 Choice of normalisation, integration and clustering parameters

The following parameter choices were made:

- **Integration / batch correction.** Harmony was retained at `theta = 2`, `sigma = 0.1`, `lambda = 1`; lower `theta` left visible per-sample sub-clusters of common lineages, higher `theta` over-mixed biologically distinct hepatocyte subtypes.
- **Filtering thresholds.** Cell-level QC retained cells with at least 4 transcripts and genes detected in at least 3 cells; `qv ≥ 20` is the Xenium recommended decoding-quality threshold.
- **Dim-reduction and UMAP.** 50 PCs were computed (elbow of the variance plot); Harmony and UMAP used the first 15 PCs (`harmony_dims = 15`, `umap_dims = 15`, `umap_neighbors = 15`, `umap_min_dist = 0.0005`).
- **Leiden resolution.** Resolutions 0.3, 0.5, 0.7 and 1.0 were inspected. 0.5 produced 21 primary clusters whose marker profiles best matched canonical liver and immune lineages; the formal stability analysis at 0.3 / 0.7 / 1.0 is reported in Supplementary Methods §1.6 and Supplementary Results §2.4.

### 1.3 Choice of cell–cell communication methods

The three methods used (CellChat v2, LIANA+ five-method consensus, COMMOT) were selected to span ligand–receptor scoring against a curated database (CellChat), expression-only consensus across heterogeneous scorers (LIANA+ wrapping NATMI, Connectome, logFC, SingleCellSignalR and CellPhoneDB) and spatial-aware diffusion-based optimal transport (COMMOT).

### 1.4 Cluster-size sensitivity of the cell–cell-communication analysis

The cluster-size filter (≥ 10 cells per sample) used in Figure 2B was probed for sensitivity by re-extracting the top-ranked coherent flows of the canonical Figure 2B set at two more conservative cell-count thresholds: ≥ 30 and ≥ 50 cells per sample per cluster. Outcomes are reported in §2.2 and tabulated in **Supplementary Table S9**.

### 1.5 Multi-test correction across CCC analyses

The CellChat coherent rule pools two complementary criteria (per-sample significance + opposite-condition exclusion) that together select for cohort-consistent differential flows. Within-sample CellChat p-values are not directly combined across samples in CellChat v2; the coherent rule is therefore a *between-sample agreement filter* rather than a statistical hypothesis test. For each coherent triple, the two per-sample CellChat permutation p-values within the enriched condition (CellChat default 100-permutation budget) were combined via Fisher's method (chi-square statistic on 2k degrees of freedom, k = 2 samples per condition; per-sample p-values floored at 1/(B + 1) to avoid -∞ on combination); the resulting per-triple condition-level p-values were then Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted across the full coherent set. Outcomes are reported in §2.3 and tabulated in **Supplementary Table S10**.

### 1.6 Clustering-stability analysis

To document the robustness of the 18 active clusters used downstream, the Leiden clustering at the chosen resolution (0.5) was compared against re-clusterings at resolutions 0.3, 0.7 and 1.0 (all run on the same Harmony-corrected first 15 principal components, k = 15 SNN graph). Three readouts were computed: (A) the cluster-to-cluster adjusted Rand index (ARI) between the chosen resolution and each alternative; (B) per-cluster median silhouette over the Harmony embedding under the chosen resolution, computed on a 30,000-cell subsample; and (C) the count of resolution-0.5 sub-clusters each resolution-0.3 cluster maps to. Outcomes are reported in §2.4, **Supplementary Figure S3** and **Supplementary Tables S12, S12b, S12c**.


## 2. Supplementary Results

### 2.1 Cross-method agreement at the lineage and L–R levels

LIANA+ and COMMOT both reproduced the dominant lineage-level architectures of Figure 2B at the cell-class level: bile-duct CD8 axis in SR-irHep and parenchymal CD4 axes in AIH (**Supplementary Tables S5–S6**; **Supplementary Fig. S1**). Of the 13 testable CellChat-coherent flows (ligand and receptor both with non-zero expression in the relevant clusters), 8 were confirmed by LIANA+ at Robust Rank Aggregation rank in the top decile of the corresponding cell-pair distribution. Of 17 L–R × direction triples testable by both CellChat and COMMOT, 9 agreed in direction; the 8 disagreements clustered on FN1 and CSF1 axes, where the longer COMMOT distance scale (250 µm diffusion) captures matrix-deposition and cytokine-diffusion patterns that lie outside the 25 µm CellChat contact range, a difference of spatial scale rather than of biological conclusion. The triple-method-confirmed core (CellChat ∩ LIANA+ ∩ COMMOT, same direction) comprised 2 of 47 CellChat-coherent triples, both CCL3/4/5 → CCR1 axes (vascular and sinusoidal endothelial → CD8T2 in SR-irHep; sinusoidal endothelial → IgM⁺ B cells in AIH); cross-method convergence is therefore at the broad lineage-level architecture rather than at individual L–R directionality.

### 2.2 Cluster-size sensitivity

The 45 coherent CellChat flows passing the n ≥ 10 per-sample filter (the display-eligible set for Figure 2B, of which the top 18 are shown) were re-extracted at the two more conservative cluster-size thresholds defined in §1.4. **7 of 8 top-ranked SR-irHep-enriched flows and 10 of 10 top-ranked AIH-enriched flows are preserved at both n ≥ 30 and n ≥ 50** (**Supplementary Table S9**). The one SR-irHep flow that drops at n ≥ 30 is an IgM⁺ B-cell → IgG⁺ plasmablast CXCL12–CXCR4 axis whose IgG⁺ plasmablast count is 28 in one sample (below n = 30). The four principal SR-irHep architectural flows (cholangiocyte → CD8T2/CD8T3 MHC-I antigen presentation, cholangiocyte → CD8T2 VEGFA → FLT1, cholangiocyte → CD8T3 CXCL9/10/11 → CXCR3) survive every higher threshold; the AIH parenchyma-centred CD4 axes (sinusoidal endothelial → CD4 CD86, hepatocyte 2 → CD4 ICOSLG → CD28 / CCR1 / CCR4, CD4 → hepatocyte 2 CD40LG–CD40) likewise survive. The CellChat "ICOS" pathway corresponds to ICOSLG → CD28 (both on the panel); ICOS itself is not on the 380-gene panel.

### 2.3 Multi-test correction

Under the Fisher-method × Benjamini–Hochberg scheme defined in §1.5, all 8 SR-irHep-enriched and all 10 AIH-enriched flows shown in Figure 2B retain BH-adjusted p < 0.10 (**Supplementary Table S10**). The orthogonal LIANA+ and COMMOT cross-validation summarised in §2.1 provides a methods-level rather than statistics-level multiplicity control.

### 2.4 Clustering stability

The broad lineage classes used in the main manuscript claims (hepatocyte, cholangiocyte, CD8 T cell, CD4 T cell, B cell, plasma cell, macrophage 1, macrophage 2, sinusoidal endothelial, vascular endothelial and stellate) are stable across Leiden resolutions 0.3, 0.7 and 1.0; within-lineage sub-cluster splits at resolution 0.5 (Hep1–4, CD8T1–3) partially merge at 0.3 and over-split at 1.0 (**Supplementary Fig. S3**; **Supplementary Tables S12, S12b, S12c**).

## 5. Supplementary Notes

### Note 1. Ig⁺ hepatocyte subset (cluster 20)

Cluster 20 (referred to here as the Ig⁺ hepatocyte subset and counted as the fifth hepatocyte state) carries hepatocyte (ARG1, SDC1, A2M, FN1) together with plasma-cell (IGHG1, IGHGP, IGKC, JCHAIN) markers, and is highly imbalanced across samples (12, 2, 243 and 478 cells in samples 18321_a, 24774_d, 54023_b and 56784_c, respectively). The most parsimonious interpretation is that the Ig⁺ signature in this hepatocyte-marker cluster reflects spatial juxtaposition between hepatocytes and the abundant infiltrating Ig⁺ plasma cells in chronically inflamed AIH tissue, that is, hepatocyte cells whose Xenium-segmented cell boundary lies close to (or in mild overlap with) a neighbouring Ig⁺ plasma cell, leading to apparent immunoglobulin transcript co-localisation at the hepatocyte. This is consistent with the strongly AIH-skewed per-sample distribution (the two AIH samples carry the bulk of the cluster, in line with their much larger plasma-cell compartment) and with the limits of cell-segmentation in lymphocyte-dense tissue. We do *not*, however, classify this cluster as clearly artefactual: low-level immunoglobulin co-expression by hepatocytes in chronic inflammation cannot be excluded from the present data, and the question would be resolved by a deeper-transcriptome panel. The cluster is excluded from the Figure 2B display by the minimum-cluster filter (n ≥ 10 cells per sample) and reported separately in Supplementary Table S4 with this caveat made explicit. The main-text cluster summary lists it among the five hepatocyte states, and it is described with this caveat in Note 3.

### Note 2. MHC-I direction convention applied to the Figure 2B Sankey

The CellChatDB-encoded MHC class I interactions (HLA-A/B/C → CD8A/CD8B) follow CellChat's universal directional rule, in which the source column is the ligand-expressing cell. Under this rule, the "source" of an MHC-I flow is the cell carrying surface MHC class I and the "target" is the CD8⁺ T cell whose CD8 co-receptor (CD8A/CD8B) engages the MHC-I peptide complex, matching the canonical immunological convention of antigen presentation (Sari & Rock, *Curr. Opin. Immunol.* **83**, 102329 (2023); [DOI: 10.1016/j.coi.2023.102329](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.coi.2023.102329)).

Because HLA-A/B/C are expressed at baseline by all nucleated cells (including CD8⁺ T cells themselves), the per-sample CellChat differential analysis occasionally returns MHC-I-pathway rows in which a CD8 cluster is assigned to the source column. Such rows do not encode a biologically meaningful "CD8 → cholangiocyte (or other partner)" antigen-presentation event; they are a numerical consequence of differential HLA-A/B/C expression entering the source-assignment rule. In these rows alone (MHC-I_CD8A and MHC-I_CD8B with CD8 in the source column), we swap source ↔ target so that the row reads presenter → CD8 T, restoring the immunologically correct direction. The redraw is applied only to MHC-I-pathway rows and only when CellChat had placed a CD8 cluster in the source column; flows with the non-T cell already in the source column are left untouched. No other CellChatDB pathway (CXCL/CCL/CSF/ICOSLG-CD28/CD86/CD40LG/FN1/VEGFA-FLT1 etc.) is modified; these accept CellChat's source → target convention verbatim. The absolute log₂ fold-change and the per-triple significance are invariant under the source ↔ target swap. Both raw and redrawn directions are reported in **Supplementary Table S5**.

### Note 3. Cluster identities and markers

The 18 active clusters (Fig. 1A,B; per-cluster markers, fold-changes and per-sample counts in Supplementary Tables S3 and S4) comprise five hepatocyte states: Hep1–3 differing in ARG1/SDC1/APOE/C1S, reported without zonal assignment because the zonation markers GLUL/AXIN2/CYP3A4/CYP2E1/HAL are not on the panel; Hep4, a proliferating MKI67/CDK1/FN1⁺ pool; and an Ig⁺ hepatocyte subset (cluster 20; ARG1/SDC1 with IGHG1/IGKC/JCHAIN), excluded from the Figure 2B display by the n ≥ 10-cells-per-sample filter (Note 1). Three CD8⁺ subclusters are resolved: CD8T1 (KLRK1/GZMA/NKG7 cytotoxic-NK-like with CXCR6/RUNX3 partial liver-retention; the panel-available multi-marker tissue-resident-memory signature does not distinguish CD8T1 from CD8T2/CD8T3; Supplementary Table S11b, Supplementary Fig. S4), CD8T2 (CD8A/KLRK1/CXCL10-high, IFN-γ-stimulated, CXCR6⁺) and CD8T3 (cycling, MKI67/CDK1⁺). The remaining clusters are one CD4⁺ T-cell cluster (IL7R/CD2⁺); cholangiocytes (CXCL6/EPCAM/SOX9); IgM⁺ B, IgG⁺ B and IgG⁺ plasmablast subsets; two macrophage states (CD163/CSF1R Kupffer-like and S100A9/ITGAX inflammatory-monocyte-like; within-lineage subtleties in Supplementary Tables S3–S4 are not used for cohort-level claims because of panel limitations); and three stromal/vascular populations (sinusoidal endothelial, vascular endothelial and stellate).
