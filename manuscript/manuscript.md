## **Abstract**

Steroid-resistant immune-checkpoint-inhibitor hepatitis (SR-irHep) can progress to bile-duct injury, yet second-line treatment remains empirical. Using 10× Xenium spatial transcriptomics, we profiled steroid-exposed SR-irHep and treatment-naïve type I autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) liver biopsies (n = 2 per condition; 146,014 cells; 380 genes). Despite lower CD8 abundance, SR-irHep showed duct-apposed CD8 cells engaging cholangiocyte MHC-I and cholangiocyte CXCL9/10/11 signalling to CD8 CXCR3, whereas AIH showed hepatocyte-apposed CD4 cells with CD86, ICOSLG-CD28 and CD40LG-CD40 costimulation. This small, unmatched discovery series offers a cellular rationale for mycophenolate and points to JAK1/2 inhibition and CXCR3 blockade as testable second-line hypotheses.

**Keywords:** steroid-resistant ICI hepatitis, vanishing bile-duct syndrome, autoimmune hepatitis, mycophenolate, ruxolitinib, spatial transcriptomics, CXCR3, cell–cell communication.

## **Main text**

Immune-checkpoint-inhibitor (ICI) hepatitis affects 5–10% of patients receiving monotherapy and about 13% receiving combination regimens, with grade ≥3 events in 1–9%^1,2^. Approximately 16% of cases are steroid-refractory (SR-irHep)^3^. A clinically distinctive subset progresses through cholangiocyte injury to bile-duct paucity^4^, overlapping ICI-induced secondary sclerosing cholangitis^5^ and contrasting with the parenchyma-centred lymphoplasmacytic inflammation of autoimmune hepatitis (AIH)^6^. Mycophenolate mofetil (MMF) is the usual empirical second-line treatment, while other agents are supported only by small series^7^; no biology-driven choice has been established.

We profiled two steroid-exposed SR-irHep biopsies and two treatment-naïve type I AIH biopsies. Both SR-irHep patients had received carboplatin, paclitaxel and pembrolizumab for triple-negative breast cancer; hepatitis arose during concurrent chemotherapy and ICI in one patient and during pembrolizumab maintenance in the other, and both were biopsied when failed response to at least 1 mg/kg/day prednisone-equivalent^7,8^ prompted second-line immunosuppression, meeting consensus criteria for steroid-refractory irAE^9^ (Supplementary Table S1). A 380-gene 10× Xenium panel retained 146,014 cells after quality control (Supplementary Table S2). Harmony-integrated clustering^10^ identified 18 active clusters after pathology-aware exclusion of a four-cell outlier, a likely segmentation-fragment cluster and a cluster confined to poorly preserved tissue: five hepatocyte states, three CD8 subclusters, CD4 cells, cholangiocytes, B-cell and plasmablast subsets, macrophages and stromal or vascular populations (Fig. 1A,B; Supplementary Note 3 and Tables S3, S4).

Condition-separated UMAPs place these clusters on the shared corrected embedding (Fig. 1B). Composition inverted between conditions (Fig. 1A, lower bars, per-sample-averaged): SR-irHep was hepatocyte-dominated, with Hep1–3 comprising 50.8% of cells versus 18.0% in AIH. Every CD8 subcluster was less abundant in SR-irHep than AIH (CD8T1, 6.6% versus 16.0%; CD8T2, 0.8% versus 2.4%; CD8T3, 0.8% versus 1.1%), as were CD4, B or plasma and macrophage populations. Cholangiocytes comprised 1.7% versus 5.3%, quantitatively accompanying bile-duct injury, although neither SR-irHep biopsy showed frank ductopenia. Because both SR-irHep biopsies followed corticosteroids, their sparse residual immune compartment cannot be read as weaker pathogenesis, as cholangitic ICI injury responds poorly to corticosteroids^11^. Representative tissue views preserve this contrast: bile-duct injury with an interface CD4/CD8 infiltrate in SR-irHep (Fig. 1C), and tertiary-lymphoid-structure-like B or plasma aggregates with parenchymal depletion in AIH (Fig. 1D).

Delaunay-graph proximity enrichment against 1,000 label permutations resolved an anatomical inversion (Fig. 2A; Supplementary Table S7). Among the ten cell pairs with the largest coherent differential enrichment, all three CD8 subclusters were closer to cholangiocytes in SR-irHep, led by IFN-γ-stimulated CD8T2 and cycling CD8T3 (Δlog₂ +2.75 and +1.83); in AIH, CD4 cells were closer to all four hepatocyte subclusters (Δlog₂ −1.4 to −2.8). A separate four-sample permutation analysis agreed in direction and rank. CD8 cells were therefore less abundant in SR-irHep yet preferentially duct-apposed, whereas AIH inflammation was organised around CD4-hepatocyte apposition.

CellChat inference within 25 µm neighbourhoods^12^ supported the same geometry (Fig. 2B), the Sankey showing the largest coherent differentials (eight SR-irHep-enriched, ten AIH-enriched flows). The strongest SR-irHep-biased flows were cholangiocyte MHC-I presentation to CD8T2 and CD8T3 through CD8A/CD8B (log₂ fold-change +8.2 and +9.0), cholangiocyte CXCL9/10/11 signalling to CXCR3 on CD8T3, and VEGFA-FLT1 onto CD8T2. CXCL9/10/11 are induced by IFN-γ through JAK1/2-STAT1^13^, and their single-cell co-expression with a cholangiocyte IFN-response composite supports, without proving, this framing, as IFNG was absent from the panel (Supplementary Fig. S5 and Tables S11c, S11c2, S11c3). Vascular endothelium also signalled to CD8T2 through CCL3/4/5-CCR1, recalling central-vein endotheliitis described mainly after CTLA-4 blockade^14^. AIH-enriched flows concentrated on parenchymal CD4 interactions: sinusoidal-endothelial CD86 and CCL/CCR4, hepatocyte ICOSLG-CD28 and CCR1 chemotaxis, hepatocyte CXCL signalling through CXCR3/CXCR6, and CD4 CD40LG to hepatocyte CD40. An AIH-enriched cholangiocyte-to-CD8T2 FN1 flow may reflect chronic duct-surface matrix. LIANA+^15^ and COMMOT^16^ showed partial lineage-level concordance but did not jointly confirm the MHC-I and CXCR3 triples (Supplementary Fig. S1; Tables S5, S6).

Representative 120 × 120 µm regions localised these results in paired H&E and segmentation views (Fig. 2C; Supplementary Fig. S2): in SR-irHep, CD8T2 and CD8T3 cells ringed injured ducts, whereas AIH ducts had fewer CD8 contacts per cholangiocyte, and CD4 cells apposed AIH hepatocytes with IL7R transcripts at the interface while remaining outside SR-irHep hepatocyte plates. These regions illustrate rather than generate the cohort statistics. The bile-duct-centred CD8 organisation resembles epithelial-bystander damage by ICI-associated cytotoxic CD8 circuits^17^ but identifies a distinct duct target, extending the histology of Sunago^4^ and Bjornsson^5^ with candidate signalling axes.

This architecture supplies a cellular rationale for MMF, which restricts activated lymphocytes dependent on de novo purine synthesis through IMPDH inhibition^18^, including the cycling CD8T3 cells at the duct interface. Among reported options, which include tacrolimus, antithymocyte globulin, tocilizumab and vedolizumab^7^, infliximab^19,20^ and ruxolitinib^21,22^, ruxolitinib intersects the inferred mechanism most directly: JAK1/2 inhibition would attenuate the IFN-γ to JAK1/2-STAT1 to CXCL9/10/11 to CXCR3 axis, though clinical experience is limited to two case reports. CXCR3 blockade is a further hypothesis, supported indirectly by attenuation of ICI myocarditis with preserved antitumour activity in a preclinical model^23^. CXCR3-related flows arose in both diseases; the discriminating feature was cholangiocyte-to-CD8 geometry in SR-irHep rather than hepatocyte-to-CD4 geometry in AIH. Translation demands caution, as CXCR3 sustains anti-PD-1 efficacy through CD8 recruitment^24^ and NK-cell infiltration of liver metastases^25^, and ruxolitinib carries infectious, cytopenic and interaction risks^26^; organ-localised or time-limited strategies warrant preclinical testing alongside emerging randomised second-line programmes^27^.

The sample size precludes population inference. Steroid exposure, cancer, prior chemotherapy and the unmatched treatment-naïve AIH comparator can all contribute to the observed differences; steroid-responsive ICI hepatitis matched for cancer, ICI and corticosteroid history would be the appropriate comparator. Prior histology supports bile-duct injury and CD8 predominance^4,5^, and a multicentre series found pembrolizumab predominance in ICI cholangiopathy^28^, but neither resolves confounding here. The targeted panel limits exhaustion, tissue-resident-memory, T-helper, zonation and cytokine resolution, and IFNG, KRT7 and KRT19 were absent; T-cell clonality and protein abundance were not measured. These communication analyses predict transcript-level spatial architecture rather than mechanism, and the MHC-I and CXCR3 findings lack convergence across every method. Independent cohorts, multiplex-protein confirmation and functional cholangiocyte-CD8 models are required. Within these constraints, bile-duct-centred CD8 engagement of cholangiocyte MHC-I and CXCR3 distinguishes SR-irHep from the hepatocyte-centred CD4 costimulation of AIH and nominates MMF, JAK1/2 inhibition and CXCR3 blockade as testable second-line hypotheses.

# Methods

## Patients, samples and ethics

Liver biopsies from four patients were profiled at the Azienda Ospedaliera Metropolitana IRCCS Ospedale Policlinico San Martino (Genoa, Italy) as a retrospective small case series: two patients with steroid-resistant immune-checkpoint-inhibitor (ICI) hepatitis (SR-irHep; sample identifiers 18321_a, 24774_d) on neoadjuvant carboplatin–paclitaxel–pembrolizumab (KEYNOTE-522 regimen) for triple-negative breast cancer (TNBC), meeting the operational definition of steroid-refractory irHep (failure of clinical or biochemical improvement after corticosteroid therapy at ≥ 1 mg/kg/day prednisone-equivalent^7,8^), and two patients with treatment-naïve autoimmune hepatitis (AIH) type I, diagnosed clinically and serologically per international AIH simplified criteria (sample identifiers 54023_b, 56784_c). Per-patient regimen timing, steroid history at biopsy, post-biopsy second-line therapy and biopsy-level histology are tabulated in Supplementary Table S1. Sample identifiers are pseudonymous codes; the linkage to the source patient is held only at the institutional level. All four patients had provided written informed consent for the research use and publication of de-identified clinical and biological material under the institutional research-consent framework in place at the Azienda Ospedaliera Metropolitana IRCCS Ospedale Policlinico San Martino. Under Italian regulations on retrospective observational research at IRCCS institutions (AIFA Determina 20 March 2008; Decreto Ministeriale 30 November 2021; DGR Liguria 858/2023), a dedicated retrospective study protocol was not required for this small case series; all procedures followed the policies of the ethics committee of Regione Liguria in the matter of publication of case reports and/or case series. Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded (FFPE) tissue blocks were sectioned at 5 µm onto Xenium slides per the 10× Genomics manufacturer's protocol.

## Xenium spatial transcriptomics

*In situ* profiling was performed on the 10× Genomics Xenium Analyzer with the 380-gene Human Immuno-Oncology Panel. Standard vendor protocols were followed for slide preparation, hybridisation, decoding, and cell segmentation (cell + nuclear boundaries from 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) plus interior cell-membrane staining). After Xenium acquisition, slides were stained with haematoxylin and eosin (H&E), and the resulting whole-slide images were registered to the Xenium coordinate space using the vendor `he_alignment/matrix.csv` affine transform; per-ROI alignment was visually verified within the 10 µm contact threshold used in Figure 2C. Per-sample output (cell-feature matrix, transcripts, cell and nucleus boundaries, DAPI and H&E pyramids, gene panel JSON) was retained in 10× standard layout. Per-sample QC metrics are reported in Supplementary Table S2; all four samples passed vendor reference ranges.

## Data processing, integration and clustering

Per-sample cell-feature matrices were imported into the Giotto framework (v4.2.2). Cell-level QC retained cells with at least 4 transcripts; genes were retained if detected in at least 3 cells; transcripts with decoding quality QV < 20 were discarded. Counts were normalised by log-transformed library-size scaling (scale factor 5,000). The four samples were embedded with principal-component analysis (PCA; 50 components computed), batch-corrected with Harmony (theta = 2, sigma = 0.1, lambda = 1) on the first 15 principal components, projected with uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP; 15 nearest neighbours, min_dist = 0.0005), and clustered with the Leiden algorithm (k = 15, resolution 0.5). Twenty-one primary clusters were obtained. Three were excluded after pathology-aware quality review: one four-cell sample-specific outlier (cluster 21); a fragment cluster with median cell area ≤ 20 µm^2^ and IGKC as the dominant detected marker, consistent with segmentation artefacts (cluster 12); and a hepatocyte-marker cluster restricted to a poorly preserved tissue region (cluster 15). Eighteen active clusters across 146,014 cells were retained. Cluster identity was assigned from per-cluster marker genes (Giotto `findMarkers_one_vs_all`, scran method, FDR < 0.05) against canonical liver-atlas signatures. Full top-5 markers and per-sample cell counts are in Supplementary Tables S3 and S4; per-marker specificity statistics are in Supplementary Table S8.

## Per-cluster marker-gene identification

For cluster characterisation, per-cluster vs. all-other-clusters differential expression was computed with Giotto's wrapper around `scran::findMarkers` on per-cell log-normalised counts (Supplementary Table S3). The top markers per cluster were used to assign cluster identity against canonical liver and immune-cell signatures; top-5 markers and per-sample cell counts are tabulated in Supplementary Tables S3 and S4.

## Spatial proximity analysis (Figure 2A)

Pairwise cluster–cluster adjacency was quantified per sample with `Giotto::cellProximityEnrichment` on a Delaunay spatial graph of cell centroids. The enrichment statistic is log₂((observed + 1) / (expected + 1)), where the expected number of neighbours is drawn from 1,000 random permutations of cluster labels within each sample. Two one-sided empirical p-values per pair (attract, avoid) were computed and Benjamini–Hochberg adjusted within sample. To accommodate the small cohort size (n = 2 per condition), a *coherent rule* was applied per direction per condition: a pair was retained when at least one sample of the condition reached padj < 0.10 in a given direction *and* no sample of the same condition crossed padj < 0.10 in the opposite direction. The condition mean log₂ enrichment was computed across the two samples of each condition; the differential Δ(SR-irHep − AIH) was used as tile colour in Figure 2A. The 10 pairs with largest |Δ| under the coherent rule are outlined in the heatmap. A permutation-based supplementary analysis (1,000 cluster-label permutations across the four samples; Supplementary Table S7) was conducted to confirm that the coherent signal is unlikely to arise by chance. Because Figure 2A and Supplementary Table S7 use different estimators (per-condition cellProximityEnrichment mean vs four-sample permutation), the two sets of Δlog₂ values agree in direction and rank but differ in absolute magnitude by design.

## Cell–cell communication (Figure 2B)

Per-sample ligand–receptor (L–R) communication probabilities were inferred with CellChat v2^12^ using a spatial neighbourhood (`contact.range` = 25 µm, `spot.size` = 10, `type = "truncatedMean"`, `trim` = 0.1) against the full CellChatDB. Three multi-ligand families whose CellChat defaults collapse mechanistically distinct receptors (MHC-I, CXCL and CCL) were ligand-split into receptor-specific axes (e.g. MHC-I_CD8A, CXCL_CXCR3, CCL_CCR1) before aggregation, yielding the receptor-anchored pathway labels shown in the Sankey. For the MHC-I family, every nucleated cell expresses HLA-A/B/C, so CellChat's source-is-ligand-expresser convention can place a CD8⁺ cluster in the source column on multi-cell-type comparisons; in MHC-I rows with a CD8 cluster as source, we swap source and target so the row reads presenter to CD8 (full description in Supplementary Note 2). Per-condition mean communication probabilities were log₂-transformed with a pseudo-count and the differential Δ(SR-irHep − AIH) computed. The same coherent rule used in the proximity analysis was applied to the L–R × cell-pair triple: a flow was retained as enriched in a condition when at least one sample of that condition reached a CellChat permutation p < 0.05 for the triple and no sample of the opposite condition did (a between-sample agreement filter, not a per-triple significance test; the separate Fisher-combined, Benjamini–Hochberg-adjusted multiplicity assessment over the coherent set is reported in Supplementary §1.5 and Table S10). Forty-seven coherent triples passed; 45 remained after the n ≥ 10 per-sample display filter (which removes two Ig⁺-hepatocyte flows). Of these, the 18 flows with the largest |log₂FC|, 8 SR-irHep-enriched and 10 AIH-enriched (up to 10 per direction), are shown in Figure 2B, with clusters of < 10 cells in any sample excluded. The full coherent set is in Supplementary Table S5.
## Orthogonal validation: LIANA+ and COMMOT

CellChat findings were cross-referenced against two independent computational approaches operating on the same per-sample data. *Expression-only consensus.* Per-sample expression-only communication scoring was performed with LIANA+^15^ using five orthogonal scoring methods (NATMI, Connectome, logFC, SingleCellSignalR and CellPhoneDB) against the LIANA Consensus database with Robust Rank Aggregation; cross-method per-pair ranks are in Supplementary Table S6. *Spatial optimal transport.* Per-sample spatial-aware CCC was computed with COMMOT^16^ using the CellChatDB ligand–receptor database, species = "human", cell-cell distance threshold `dis_thr` = 250 µm and heteromeric receptor handling enabled. COMMOT was run on each sample individually (the largest sample, 54023_b, with 52,266 cells × 33 testable L–R pairs, was the dominant computational cost). Per-sample scores were aggregated to per-condition differentials with explicit three-layer handling of structural missingness (presence-only versus testable-zero versus untested); details are in the Supplementary Methods.

## Software, data and code availability

All analyses were performed in R 4.5.2 (Giotto 4.2.2, GiottoClass, CellChat 2.x, scran, ggalluvial, ggplot2, cowplot, magick, FNN, arrow, data.table) and Python 3.10+ (commot 0.0.3, anndata, tifffile, scipy.ndimage, PIL). Conda environment specifications for both languages and an end-to-end pipeline launcher are deposited with the analysis repository. Random seeds are fixed for the Leiden clustering, the proximity-permutation step and ROI arrow jitter; all other steps are deterministic.
